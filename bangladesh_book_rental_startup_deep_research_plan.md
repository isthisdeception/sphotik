# Bangladesh Nationwide Book-Rental Startup
## Deep Research, Market-Test, Pricing, Website, Operations, and Growth Plan

**Prepared for:** Sourov Biswas  
**Research date:** 4 August 2026  
**Market:** Bangladesh  
**Stage:** Idea validation / pre-launch  
**Main goal:** Test the idea with minimum spending, learn quickly, and grow only after the numbers work.

---

## 1. Executive Verdict

The idea is worth testing, but the safest first version is **not** a full nationwide library with a large owned inventory.

The best first version is:

> A small, membership-based book-rental service that starts with one controlled customer group, buys books only after real demand appears, and uses a transparent request/procurement system.

### My overall view

The startup can work because:

- Many people want to read more books but do not want to buy every book.
- Some books are expensive, difficult to find locally, or only needed once.
- Facebook and Messenger/WhatsApp can make customer acquisition easy to test.
- A refundable deposit can reduce loss risk.
- A digital catalog and courier delivery can reach readers beyond a physical library.

However, there are serious challenges:

1. **Two-way delivery is expensive.**
   The customer must pay for both sending and returning the books. Outside Dhaka, one full borrowing cycle may cost more than the rental value of a single low-priced book.

2. **Buying every requested book can consume cash quickly.**
   If the first 50 members each request a different book, the business may have to buy 50 books before any book is reused.

3. **A refundable deposit is not business income.**
   It must remain available for refund and should not be used as normal operating cash.

4. **The catalog must be honest about availability.**
   Books that you do not own should not appear as immediately available. They should be marked as **Procurement**, **Request only**, or **Waitlist**.

5. **Retention matters more than sign-ups.**
   A customer who joins but borrows only once is less useful than a customer who borrows every month and recommends the service.

### Recommended launch position

Start with:

- University students and young professionals
- Bangla and English fiction, self-development, business, biography, and popular nonfiction
- One launch area or community
- Campus or neighborhood pickup points where possible
- A 30-member paid pilot
- A 90-day founding membership
- Manual operations before courier API automation

Because you are connected to Daffodil International University, a DIU-centered pilot gives you an unusually cheap starting point. You can personally reach students, use hall/campus pickup points, collect fast feedback, and reduce delivery costs.

## 2. Important Market Correction: You Have More Than One Competitor

Bishwa Sahitya Kendra is a major alternative, but it is not the only relevant competitor.

### Direct and indirect competitors

| Competitor or alternative | Model | Main strength | Weakness or gap you may target |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bishwa Sahitya Kendra Mobile Library | Low-cost membership, mobile library vehicles, scheduled local stops | Very low cost, strong reputation, wide national reach | Customers must follow routes and schedules; catalog and doorstep experience are different |
| Bookcentric | Commercial subscription-based book rental with online catalog and delivery | Closest direct competitor; clear plans and online process | Limited service areas shown during registration; room for stronger request-based catalog and nationwide bundle model |
| Boipathok | App-based rental model reported with annual fee and delivery | Low annual price and simple value proposition | Public information appears limited and service coverage has been Dhaka-focused |
| Public and university libraries | Physical borrowing | Very low cost | Fixed location, limited hours, variable catalog, no doorstep convenience |
| Facebook book groups | Buy/sell/exchange | Strong communities and low-cost used books | No standard subscription, condition control, due-date management, or guaranteed process |
| Rokomari and bookstores | Purchase | Large catalog and ownership | Expensive for one-time reading |
| E-books, PDFs, and piracy | Instant access | Very cheap or free | Copyright, reading preference, screen fatigue, and limited physical-book experience |

### What this means

Do not market the startup as only “another library.”

Your stronger position should be:

> **Request almost any book, borrow without buying, receive it through a simple tracked process, and return it when finished.**

Your biggest differentiation can be a combination of:

- Demand-based procurement
- Transparent request voting
- Better digital experience
- Doorstep or pickup-point delivery
- Clear condition and refund rules
- Member recommendations and reading community
- Coverage expansion based on real demand

## 3. Market Reality and Demand Hypothesis

There is no reliable public number for the exact size of the paid physical-book rental market in Bangladesh. Therefore, the market should be validated through real customer behavior, not through broad e-commerce statistics.

Useful market signals include:

- Bangladesh has a large internet and mobile-internet population.
- Facebook has tens of millions of users in Bangladesh and is a practical place to test demand.
- Bookcentric currently offers paid book-rental subscriptions.
- Bishwa Sahitya Kendra continues to operate a large mobile-library network.
- Online bookstores and book communities show that readers are comfortable discovering books digitally.

These signals show that the idea is plausible. They do **not** prove that a nationwide paid courier model will be profitable.

### The five assumptions you must test

1. People will pay a membership fee instead of only buying used books.
2. People will also accept paying two-way delivery charges.
3. Members will borrow repeatedly, not only once.
4. Requested books will be reused by several members.
5. Loss, damage, late returns, and support costs will remain low.

The pilot must test these assumptions before heavy investment.

## 4. Best Initial Customer Segment

Trying to serve every reader at launch will make marketing expensive and operations confusing.

### Recommended first segment

**University students and young professionals aged roughly 18–35 who already read at least one physical book every one or two months.**

Typical interests:

- Bangla fiction
- English fiction
- Self-development
- Business and entrepreneurship
- Biography
- Technology and career books
- Popular psychology
- Competitive exam or skill books only when they can be reused often

### Why this segment is suitable

- Easy to reach through Facebook, university groups, book groups, and referrals
- Comfortable with online ordering and mobile payments
- Often price-sensitive
- More likely to compare rental cost with purchase cost
- Can use campus pickup points
- Can create social content, reviews, and referrals

### Segments to avoid initially

- Rare imported books
- Very expensive hardcovers
- Academic textbooks that change by edition
- Exam guidebooks with a short seasonal life
- Children’s books until you have a strong damage-control process
- Collectors who care about perfect condition
- Customers who need same-day or guaranteed urgent availability

### Future segments

After the first model works, test separately:

1. Children’s monthly book boxes
2. Corporate employee libraries
3. Hostel or apartment pickup libraries
4. School reading programs
5. Premium imported-book membership
6. Used-book buyback and exchange

Do not mix all these models in the first pilot.

## 5. Recommended Business Model

## 5.1 Subscription is suitable, but it should not be unlimited

A subscription gives predictable revenue and encourages repeated borrowing. However, an unlimited plan can be abused and makes costs unpredictable.

Use:

- Maximum books at one time
- Monthly borrowing-credit limit
- Book-value limit
- Clear loan duration
- Renewal rules
- Actual delivery charged separately

### Recommended credit rule

- One normal book uses one credit.
- A high-value or oversized book may use two credits.
- Members cannot borrow a new book while an overdue book is active.
- Unused credits should expire monthly during the pilot; carrying them forward creates accounting and capacity problems.
- A replacement shipment after a wrong or damaged delivery should not consume a credit.

## 5.2 Use three catalog availability levels

Your plan to list more than 1,000 books before buying them is useful for demand discovery, but the website must be transparent.

### Status A — In Stock

- You currently own a copy.
- It can normally be dispatched within 24–48 hours.
- The page shows the number of available copies or the expected wait.

### Status B — Procurement

- You do not currently own the book.
- You believe it can be purchased from a supplier.
- The customer can request it.
- The website shows an estimated procurement time, such as 3–7 working days.
- The order is confirmed only after you verify price and availability.

### Status C — Request Only / Waitlist

- Availability or price is uncertain.
- The member can vote or join the waitlist.
- No dispatch promise is made.
- You buy it only when demand or expected reuse is strong enough.

### Never use misleading labels

Do not show an unowned book as “Available now.”  
Do not accept normal book-payment or promise immediate delivery before confirming procurement.

## 5.3 Demand-based buying rules

A book should normally be purchased when one or more of these are true:

- At least two members have requested it.
- It is a popular title with a strong chance of at least three rentals in six months.
- The purchase price is low compared with the membership revenue it can support.
- It fills a clear gap in a high-demand category.
- A founding member requests it and the book is within the pilot’s approved budget.
- A supplier offers a strong discount.
- It can be resold later with limited loss.

Avoid purchasing when:

- Only one person wants a very expensive title.
- The book is unlikely to be requested again.
- Its replacement value is above the member’s deposit or plan limit.
- The edition is likely to become outdated.
- The procurement time is too long.
- The book is fragile, rare, or difficult to replace.

## 6. Recommended Pricing

Pricing below is a **launch hypothesis**, not a final answer. It must be tested using real borrowing behavior and actual book-acquisition cost.

The prices are designed to stay reasonably close to the current commercial market while allowing more safety for a new demand-based service.

## 6.1 Founding pilot

### Founding 90-Day Plan

| Item | Recommendation |
|---|---:|
| Membership fee | **৳699 for 90 days** |
| Refundable security deposit | **৳1,000** |
| Books at one time | 1 |
| Maximum borrowing | 2 books per month |
| Normal loan period | 21 days |
| Renewal | One 7-day renewal if nobody is waiting |
| Delivery and return | Paid by member at actual cost |
| Book-value limit | Normally up to ৳700 replacement value |
| Initial capacity | First 30 paid members |
| Founding benefit | Locked discounted renewal for the first year or a small referral credit |

Do not begin with hundreds of paid members. Thirty members are enough to reveal the main operational problems.

## 6.2 Regular plans after the pilot

| Plan | Six months | One year | Deposit | At one time | Monthly credits | Best for |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **Lite** | ৳1,899 | ৳3,490 | ৳1,000 | 1 book | 2 | Occasional readers |
| **Reader** | ৳3,599 | ৳6,490 | ৳1,500 | 2 books | 4 | Regular readers |
| **Family** | ৳4,999 | ৳8,990 | ৳2,500 | 3 books | 5 | Shared household use |

### Important pricing notes

- The yearly plan gives roughly a 8–10% discount compared with buying two half-year plans.
- Do not offer a very cheap yearly-only plan before validating retention.
- Keep a 90-day trial so customers can test trust and delivery.
- Delivery is not included in these prices.
- A premium-title top-up may be needed when replacement value exceeds the normal plan limit.
- One member account should not be shared outside the registered household.
- Prices should be reviewed after the first 100 completed loans.

## 6.3 Why not make it cheaper?

A subscription can look profitable until members use all their borrowing credits.

Example full-use annual revenue per allowed book:

| Plan | Annual fee | Maximum annual books | Revenue per allowed book |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Lite | ৳3,490 | 24 | About ৳145 |
| Reader | ৳6,490 | 48 | About ৳135 |
| Family | ৳8,990 | 60 | About ৳150 |

This revenue must cover:

- Book depreciation
- Packaging
- Inspection and cleaning
- Payment costs
- Support work
- Loss and damage reserve
- Storage
- Software and messaging
- Taxes
- Future staff cost

A lower price can work only when books are reused many times, average member usage is lower than the maximum, and operational costs remain small.

## 7. Unit Economics

The purpose of unit economics is to answer:

> How much money remains after one borrowing cycle?

## 7.1 Example cost per loan

Assumptions:

- Average book purchase cost: ৳350
- Useful rentals before retirement or resale: 8
- Delivery is paid separately by the customer

| Cost item | Example cost per completed loan |
|---|---:|
| Book depreciation: ৳350 ÷ 8 | ৳44 |
| Reusable/normal packaging allocation | ৳12 |
| Inspection and cleaning | ৳12 |
| Loss and damage reserve | ৳15 |
| Payment and customer-support allocation | ৳15 |
| Storage and basic operations allocation | ৳10 |
| **Estimated variable cost per loan** | **৳108** |

At maximum plan use, revenue per allowed loan is around ৳135–৳150. This leaves only about ৳27–৳42 before advertising, founder salary, tax, major damage, failed delivery, and overhead.

Therefore:

- Do not start with heavy discounts.
- Track every cost.
- Encourage two-book bundles.
- Buy books that can be reused.
- Use pickup points whenever possible.
- Raise prices if contribution per loan remains below 25%.
- Do not include free nationwide delivery at launch.

## 7.2 Sensitivity to book reuse

| Purchase cost | Useful rentals | Book cost per rental |
|---:|---:|---:|
| ৳350 | 4 | ৳88 |
| ৳350 | 6 | ৳58 |
| ৳350 | 8 | ৳44 |
| ৳350 | 10 | ৳35 |
| ৳500 | 6 | ৳83 |
| ৳500 | 8 | ৳63 |

This table shows why repeat demand matters. A book borrowed only once or twice is not an asset; it is trapped cash.

## 7.3 Working-capital examples

Assume average purchase cost is ৳350.

| Pilot size | Average unique new books needed per member | Possible initial book purchases |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 30 members | 1.5 | ৳15,750 |
| 50 members | 1.5 | ৳26,250 |
| 100 members | 1.5 | ৳52,500 |
| 100 members | 2.0 | ৳70,000 |

This is why the first cohort should be limited.

### Security-deposit rule

Record the deposit as a **liability**, not revenue.

Recommended practice:

- Keep deposit money in a separate bank or mobile-financial account or at least a separate ledger.
- Never include it when calculating profit.
- Refund it after all books, late fees, and damage claims are cleared.
- Publish the expected refund time.
- Keep transaction evidence.
- Refund through the original payment method when practical.

## 8. Delivery Economics and Courier Strategy

Current public courier prices show that two-way delivery is one of the biggest risks.

Typical public rates vary by weight and route. For example, Pathao’s published May 2026 rate starts around:

- Inside Dhaka: ৳60–৳90 for parcels up to 2 kg
- Dhaka to outside Dhaka: ৳110–৳170
- Outside-Dhaka intercity: approximately ৳120–৳180

A full borrowing cycle requires both outbound and return movement. The customer may therefore pay roughly:

- Inside Dhaka: around ৳120–৳180 total before other charges
- Dhaka to another district: around ৳220–৳340 total before other charges

Actual reverse-delivery, COD, VAT, remote-area, weight, and negotiated merchant charges may differ.

## 8.1 Operational recommendation

### Phase 1 — Manual courier booking

For the first 30–50 members:

- Compare Pathao, Steadfast, eCourier, REDX, and one local option.
- Open merchant accounts.
- Book deliveries manually from the merchant dashboard.
- Record courier ID and tracking link in your admin panel.
- Do not spend development time on APIs yet.
- Test failed delivery, return pickup, damaged parcel, and claim handling.

### Phase 2 — One courier integration

After at least 50 successful shipments:

- Integrate the courier with the best real performance.
- Keep another courier as backup.
- Do not integrate all couriers at once.
- Use API webhooks or scheduled tracking updates.
- Store raw courier status and map it to your own status.

### Courier selection scorecard

Evaluate each courier using real data:

| Factor | Suggested weight |
|---|---:|
| Successful delivery rate | 25% |
| Return-pickup reliability | 20% |
| Damage/loss handling | 15% |
| Actual cost | 15% |
| Coverage | 10% |
| API quality | 5% |
| Support response | 5% |
| Settlement speed | 5% |

## 8.2 Make shipping less painful

- Encourage two books in one shipment.
- Offer campus, hostel, café, or neighborhood pickup points.
- Set one or two dispatch days per week during the pilot.
- Use lightweight reusable mailers.
- Let the customer request return pickup from the dashboard.
- Consider a prepaid delivery wallet later.
- Use 30-day loans for long-distance bundle shipments.
- Do not offer cash-on-delivery for refundable deposits unless necessary.
- Collect membership and deposit before first dispatch.

## 8.3 Recommended loan periods

| Delivery mode | Recommended period |
|---|---|
| Campus or pickup point | 21 days |
| Inside launch city | 21 days |
| Inter-district shipment | 30 days |
| Renewal | One additional 7 days if no waitlist |
| Grace period | 3 days after due date |

A return should count as on time when the member requests pickup or hands the book to the courier by the due date, not when it reaches your office.

## 9. Membership, Return, Damage, and Refund Policies

Policies must be short, visible, and easy to understand.

## 9.1 Membership policy

- Membership begins after payment and identity/contact verification.
- Delivery charges are separate unless a promotion clearly says otherwise.
- Borrowing credits reset each month.
- Membership time continues while the customer holds a book.
- Membership may be paused only for verified service failure, not normal non-use.
- The business may refuse very high-value or unavailable requests.
- The member must keep contact and delivery information updated.

## 9.2 Late-return policy

Recommended pilot rule:

- Reminder 3 days before due date
- Reminder on due date
- 3-day grace period
- After grace: ৳5 per day per book
- Late fee capped at the lower of a fixed limit or part of the replacement cost
- New borrowing blocked until return
- Repeated late return may reduce borrowing limit or end membership

The goal is to recover the book, not to earn from penalties.

## 9.3 Damage grading

### Normal wear

No charge for:

- Small edge wear
- Normal page aging
- Light cover marks
- Minor binding wear caused by normal reading

### Chargeable damage

Possible charge for:

- Torn or missing pages
- Water damage
- Writing or highlighting
- Strong stains or smell
- Broken binding caused by misuse
- Lost book
- Unreturned accessories

### Evidence process

- Photograph the book before dispatch.
- Record condition grade.
- Let the customer report an existing problem within 24 hours of delivery.
- Photograph the returned book during inspection.
- Give the customer evidence before deducting anything.
- Offer an appeal channel.

## 9.4 Lost-book rule

Use replacement cost, not an arbitrary punishment.

Recommended charge:

> Current replacement price + direct procurement cost + any unpaid late fee, minus any recoverable resale value where appropriate.

Never charge more without a clear written basis.

## 9.5 Deposit refund

Recommended promise:

- Customer requests exit.
- All borrowed books must be returned.
- Final inspection and account review take place.
- Any supported charge is explained.
- Remaining deposit is refunded within 7–10 working days.
- Refund confirmation is sent by SMS, email, or WhatsApp.

## 11. Payment Strategy

A full payment gateway can have setup and transaction costs. For a small pilot, it may be unnecessary.

### Pilot payment flow

1. Customer submits membership application.
2. System creates a payment reference.
3. Customer pays through an approved merchant payment channel.
4. Admin verifies the payment.
5. Membership and deposit are recorded separately.
6. Receipt is sent.
7. First borrowing becomes available.

Use a proper merchant account, not an unclear personal payment process, when possible.

### Later payment integration

Add a gateway after the model has proven demand and the saved admin time justifies the cost.

Needed payment types:

- Membership
- Deposit
- Delivery
- Premium-title top-up
- Late or damage charge
- Deposit refund
- Membership renewal

### Accounting rule

Each payment must be split into the correct category. For example:

| Customer payment | Accounting treatment |
|---|---|
| Membership fee | Revenue, subject to applicable rules |
| Refundable deposit | Liability |
| Delivery collected for courier | Pass-through or revenue/expense based on accounting treatment |
| Damage recovery | Recovery/other income or asset adjustment |
| Deposit refund | Reduction of deposit liability |

Ask a Bangladesh accountant to confirm tax, VAT/BIN, and accounting treatment before launch.

> Facebook ad or organic post → Landing page → Qualified waitlist → WhatsApp/phone confirmation → Paid founding membership → First borrowing → Return → Second borrowing → Review/referral

## 12.4 What to measure

Do not judge success by reach or likes.

Track:

| Metric | Early target or decision rule |
|---|---|
| Landing-page conversion to qualified lead | Aim for 10% or more |
| Qualified lead to paid pilot member | Aim for 15% or more |
| Overall visitor to paid member | 3–5% is a useful early target |
| Cost per paid pilot member | Preferably no more than about ৳300–৳400, or below three months of expected contribution |
| First borrowing within 14 days | More than 60% |
| Second borrowing within 45 days | More than 50% |
| On-time return | More than 90% |
| Loss or serious damage | Below 3% |
| Support complaint resolved | Within 72 hours |
| Member recommendation | Majority should say they would recommend it |

These are management targets, not industry facts.

## 12.5 Organic growth plan

After the first paid cohort:

- Publish short member reviews.
- Post “buy versus borrow” cost examples.
- Share newly available books.
- Let followers vote on books to purchase.
- Create monthly reading challenges.
- Feature member reading photos with permission.
- Use campus ambassadors.
- Give a referral reward only after the referred member pays.
- Partner with book reviewers.
- Interview local authors.
- Publish SEO-friendly book and author pages.
- Build email, SMS, Messenger, or WhatsApp reminder lists with consent.
- Create district waitlists before opening service there.
- Share transparent behind-the-scenes procurement and inspection content.

Do not buy fake followers or use page likes as the main success measure.

## 13. Low-Cost Market Validation Plan

## Phase 0 — Founder preparation: 3–5 days

Complete:

- Working name
- One-sentence promise
- Target customer
- Pilot service area
- Draft policies
- Pilot plan and price
- Maximum 30 members
- Book-buying budget
- Courier shortlist
- Simple financial spreadsheet
- Landing-page copy
- Customer-interview form

## Phase 1 — Problem interviews: Week 1

Interview at least 30 suitable readers.

Do not begin by selling the idea. Ask about current behavior.

### Interview questions

1. Which physical books did you read in the last three months?
2. Did you buy, borrow, or download them?
3. How much did you spend?
4. Which book did you want but not buy, and why?
5. How often do you finish a book?
6. What happens to books after you read them?
7. Have you used a library or rental service?
8. Would you pay delivery in both directions?
9. What deposit would feel acceptable?
10. Would you prefer one book for 21 days or two books for 30 days?
11. Which five books would you request first?
12. What would make you distrust this service?
13. What would make you recommend it?

### Strong demand signals

- The person names recent books without needing suggestions.
- They already spend money on books.
- They complain about price, storage, or availability.
- They accept a deposit.
- They accept actual delivery cost.
- They provide specific requested titles.
- They agree to join a paid pilot rather than only saying the idea is good.

## Phase 2 — Landing-page smoke test: Week 2

Build only:

- Home/landing page
- How it works
- Pilot price
- Book-request search/catalog
- Waitlist form
- FAQ
- Policies
- Contact/WhatsApp
- Analytics

List 1,000+ titles only if each has an honest availability label.

### Success condition

Proceed to the paid pilot when you have:

- At least 100 qualified leads, or
- At least 30 people ready to pay the pilot fee and deposit

A large number of free sign-ups with no willingness to pay is not enough.

## Phase 3 — Concierge pilot: Weeks 3–8

Run manually with 30 paid members.

Manual tasks are acceptable:

- Verify payment
- Approve title
- Buy book
- Add barcode
- Photograph condition
- Pack
- Book courier
- Send tracking
- Remind due date
- Arrange return
- Inspect
- Update spreadsheet/admin panel
- Record cost and feedback

The purpose is learning, not automation.

## Phase 4 — Controlled expansion: Weeks 9–12

Expand to 50–100 members only when:

- Delivery process works
- Deposit records are correct
- Return rate is strong
- Book reuse is visible
- Support is manageable
- Members borrow again
- Unit economics are not negative

Add one feature or automation at a time.

## 14. Go, Fix, or Stop Decision

## GO

Continue and invest gradually when:

- At least 30 customers pay, not only register
- More than 50% complete a second borrowing within 45 days
- More than 90% return on time
- Serious loss or damage remains below 3%
- Cost to acquire a customer is recoverable within around three months of contribution
- Popular books are rented repeatedly
- Delivery cost does not stop most customers
- At least 30% of new members begin coming through referral or organic channels over time
- Customers understand the deposit and refund rules
- Support workload is manageable

## FIX

Change price, segment, or process when:

- People join but do not borrow
- Many requests are for unique high-value books
- Delivery complaints are common
- First borrowing is slow
- Repeat borrowing is between 25% and 50%
- Members do not understand credits
- Packaging or courier damage is too high
- You are using deposits to finance operations
- Admin work is too heavy

Possible fixes:

- Use pickup points
- Serve one city
- Send two-book bundles
- Increase loan duration
- Increase membership price
- Limit premium titles
- Buy only titles with multiple requests
- Negotiate publisher discounts
- Simplify plans
- Improve onboarding

## STOP OR PIVOT

Pause the original model when:

- Fewer than 10 people pay after 100 genuinely qualified leads
- Most interested people refuse two-way delivery
- Second borrowing stays below 25%
- Loss or serious damage exceeds 5%
- Most requested books are expensive and never reused
- Customer-acquisition cost cannot be recovered
- Deposit obligations create cash stress
- Courier failures make the promise unreliable

Possible pivots:

- Campus pickup library
- Children’s monthly bundle
- Corporate library service
- Hostel/apartment reading club
- Used-book exchange
- Book-resale membership
- District partner or franchise pickup points

## 17. Legal and Compliance Checklist for Bangladesh

This is a business planning checklist, not legal advice.

Before public launch, check:

- Business structure, initially possibly sole proprietorship
- Trade license
- e-TIN
- Business bank account
- Merchant mobile-financial-service account
- DBID or other digital-commerce registration requirements
- BIN/VAT applicability
- Invoice and receipt process
- Consumer complaint channel
- Terms of service
- Privacy policy
- Cookie and marketing consent where applicable
- Membership cancellation
- Deposit refund
- Stock and procurement disclosure
- Delivery timeline
- Refund timeline
- Data-security process
- Courier contract
- Supplier invoices
- Copyright-safe use of book covers and descriptions
- Accounting treatment of deposits
- Record retention

### Important digital-commerce rules to design around

The Bangladesh Digital Commerce Operational Guidelines require clear disclosure of service terms, identity and registration information, product/service details, stock status, delivery, complaints, and refunds.

For your model, this means:

- Clearly mark books that are not in stock.
- Do not promise normal immediate delivery for request-only books.
- Show expected procurement and delivery time.
- Give customers a complaint channel.
- Keep invoices and transaction records.
- Publish refund and cancellation rules.
- Resolve complaints promptly.
- Confirm current tax, DBID, and registration requirements before collecting money.

## 18. Operations Manual for Every Book

## 18.1 Procurement

1. Request reaches threshold.
2. Admin checks supplier, edition, price, and delivery time.
3. Expected reuse is estimated.
4. Purchase is approved within budget.
5. Member receives confirmation.
6. Book is bought with invoice.
7. Catalog status changes.

## 18.2 Receiving

1. Verify title, edition, and condition.
2. Record purchase cost.
3. Add unique copy ID.
4. Attach barcode/QR label.
5. Photograph cover, spine, and key condition areas.
6. Set condition grade.
7. Add protective cover if needed.
8. Store in assigned location.

## 18.3 Outbound

1. Confirm active membership and deposit.
2. Confirm no overdue book.
3. Reserve copy.
4. Inspect and photograph.
5. Pack.
6. Create courier shipment.
7. Send tracking and due-date information.
8. Start loan period from confirmed delivery.

## 18.4 Return

1. Member requests return.
2. System calculates return method and charge.
3. Courier pickup or pickup-point return is created.
4. Tracking is monitored.
5. Book arrives.
6. Staff records opening video/photo for disputed cases.
7. Book is inspected.
8. Condition is compared.
9. Charge or no-charge decision is recorded.
10. Copy returns to available, repair, or retired status.
11. Member receives completion message.

## 21. Risks and Controls

| Risk | Early control |
|---|---|
| Customers dislike two-way delivery cost | Pickup points, bundles, city-first launch |

| Supplier stock changes | Confirm before promising |

## Closing Recommendation

Do not begin by building the largest catalog, integrating every courier, or announcing nationwide service.

Begin by proving this small loop:

> A real reader pays → requests a book → accepts delivery cost → receives the correct book → returns it on time → borrows again → recommends another member.

When that loop works repeatedly and profitably, expansion becomes much safer.
